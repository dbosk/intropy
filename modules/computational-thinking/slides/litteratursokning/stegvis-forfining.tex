\section{Research Context}
Research question: Is stepwise refinement (successively refining named
steps into more detailed instructions until everything can be expressed
in the programming language) an established method for program
development, and does it originate with Niklaus Wirth (Program
Development by Stepwise Refinement, CACM 1971)?

A paper is RELEVANT if it describes, formalises, applies, teaches,
evaluates or criticises stepwise refinement, top-down design, or
structured programming AS A PROGRAM-DEVELOPMENT METHOD, or if it bears
on where the method came from.

Assign exactly one of these four category tags:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  supports-claim: bears on the claim AND supports that stepwise
  refinement is an established method. Its original statement, later
  formalisation (refinement calculus, correctness-preserving
  refinement), textbook or curricular status, documented adoption, or
  living descendants of the method.
\item
  qualifies-claim: bears on the claim BUT contradicts or narrows it.
  Evidence that purely functional top-down decomposition is not the best
  modularisation (information hiding), that the systematic derivation of
  a program from requirements is an idealisation that must be faked
  afterwards, or any other criticism of stepwise refinement's scope,
  teachability or effectiveness.
\item
  adjacent-subtopic: computer science or software engineering work with
  NO bearing on whether stepwise refinement is established or where it
  came from. Model refinement in an unrelated formal sense, verification
  tooling, program synthesis, general software-process work, stepwise
  procedures in unrelated systems.
\item
  off-topic-false-hit: not about program development at all. Grain
  refinement in metallurgy, refinement of chemical or biological
  processes, mathematics unrelated to programs, medicine, or any other
  unrelated use of the words ``refinement'', ``stepwise'', or
  ``structured''.
\end{itemize}
